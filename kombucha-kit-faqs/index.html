Kombucha kit questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

A kombucha brewing kit typically includes a glass jar, breathable cloth, rubber band, starter tea or SCOBY, and sometimes pH strips. The best kit for you depends on your batch size, budget, and how much hands-on maintenance you want. Look for wide-mouth jars, organic tea, and a reliable source for SCOBY.

What should a quality kombucha brewing kit include?

A well-rounded kit usually starts with a glass jar, often a gallon size, though half-gallon and larger options exist. The jar should have a wide mouth so you can easily reach in to clean and remove the SCOBY. Most kits also provide a breathable cloth cover (cotton or muslin) and a rubber band or string to secure it, which keeps out fruit flies and dust while allowing airflow. Beyond the vessel, many kits come with a dehydrated or live SCOBY (symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeast) and a bit of starter tea to lower the pH of your first batch. Some include pH test strips, organic tea bags, and even sugar, though you can source those yourself. A quality kit will also offer clear brewing instructions, either printed or via a video link. Avoid kits that use plastic or metal lids for the brewing phase, anything that restricts airflow or reacts with the acidic brew can cause problems.

How do I choose the right jar size and shape?

The most common starter size is one gallon, which yields roughly half a gallon of finished kombucha per batch after you reserve starter tea. That’s enough for moderate consumption. If you plan to drink a lot or share, look for half-gallon or larger jars, many continuous brew setups use vessels from one to three gallons. Shape matters more than you might think. Tall, narrow jars can make it hard to submerge the SCOBY evenly and may slow fermentation. A squat, wide jar gives the SCOBY more surface area, which helps it breathe and ferment more consistently. Wide mouths also make cleaning easier. If you choose a jar with a spigot, ensure it’s stainless steel or food-grade plastic, and be prepared to clean it thoroughly after each batch to prevent clogs and contamination.

What's the difference between a continuous brew kit and a batch brew kit?

A batch brew kit is the simplest setup: you brew a full batch, bottle it, clean the jar, and start over. This gives you complete control over each batch and is more forgiving for beginners. Most starter kits are batch brew, and they’re easy to store and maintain. Continuous brew kits have a spigot at the bottom and a lid with an airlock. You feed the jar with fresh sweet tea as you draw off finished kombucha from the tap. These kits require more initial setup and careful balance but can produce a steady stream of kombucha with less day-to-day work. They’re best for people who brew regularly and want to avoid cleaning a jar every week or two. However, if something goes wrong, you can lose a larger volume of tea, so they’re less beginner-friendly.

Do I need a temperature control accessory for my kit?

Kombucha ferments best between 75 and 85 degrees Fahrenheit. If your kitchen runs cooler than that, especially in winter, fermentation can slow to a crawl, increasing the risk of mold. A heating wrap or a temperature-controlled brewing pad can keep the jar cozy and consistent. Some kits include a wrap, or you can buy one separately for a moderate additional cost. That said, many brewers manage fine without one if they keep the jar in a warm spot away from drafts, like the top of the refrigerator or a pantry near the stove. If you’re just starting, try a batch without added heat. If you see little activity after several days, consider a heating wrap. Avoid placing the jar in direct sunlight, as UV light can damage the culture and create off flavors.

How do I keep my kombucha kit clean and avoid mold?

Cleanliness is the single most important habit for successful brewing. Always wash your hands before handling the SCOBY or jar. Wash the jar and all equipment with hot water and a mild, unscented soap, or better yet, use plain hot water and a scrub brush. Rinse thoroughly. Never use antibacterial soap or bleach, as residues can kill your culture. Mold usually appears as fuzzy patches on the surface, often green, black, or blue. It’s almost always caused by low acidity, cool temperatures, or contamination. To prevent it, always keep your jar covered with a breathable cloth and starter tea at a low pH (below 4.5). If you see mold, discard the entire batch, do not try to rescue it. Clean the jar very well and start fresh with a new SCOBY. Good airflow and consistent temperature are your best defenses.

Frequently asked questions

Can I brew kombucha in a plastic container?

You can, but glass is strongly preferred. Plastic can develop scratches that harbor bacteria, and over time it may leach chemicals into the acidic brew. Food-grade plastic buckets are sometimes used for large continuous brews, but glass stays cleaner and lets you watch your SCOBY grow.

How often should I wash the jar between batches?

Wash the jar with hot water and a soft scrub after every batch. If you use starter tea from the previous batch, you can often skip a deep clean for a few cycles, but a full wash every other batch helps prevent off flavors. Never use soap that leaves a residue.

What type of tea is best for beginners?

Start with plain black or green tea, no oils, flavors, or herbal blends. The SCOBY needs the caffeine and tannins to thrive. Once you have a few successful batches, you can experiment with blends, but keep the base at least 75 percent true tea for consistent results.

Do I need to buy a SCOBY separately if I get a kit?

Most complete kits include a dehydrated or live SCOBY with starter tea. Some kits assume you already have a SCOBY and sell just the jar and cloth, read the product description carefully. If you’re a total beginner, look for a kit that includes the culture so you can start your first batch right away.

How do I know when my kombucha is ready to bottle?

Taste is the best test. After about seven to fourteen days, begin tasting daily with a clean straw or spoon. When it shifts from sweet tea to pleasantly tart and slightly fizzy, it’s ready. The pH should be between 2.5 and 4.5, but taste is more reliable than any number.

Can I reuse the cloth covers that come with the kit?

Yes, as long as you wash them between batches. Use hot water and a mild detergent; avoid fabric softeners that can leave chemical residue. If the cloth develops holes or stains, replace it, cheesecloth from a grocery store or a dedicated kombucha cover works fine. Some kits include extra cloths.

In shortChoosing the right kombucha brewing kit comes down to understanding your brewing environment and how much involvement you want. Start with a simple glass jar kit and a healthy SCOBY, keep your setup clean and warm, and you’ll be sipping homemade kombucha in a few weeks. As you gain confidence, you can explore extra accessories like a heat wrap or a continuous brew vessel, but don’t overcomplicate your first batch.
